# Survey About LLM Agents and Tool Use

## TL;DR
- LLM agents leverage AI capabilities to autonomously perceive their environment and act upon it, with their architectures often including memory and planning modules [1][2].
- Key methodologies for integrating tools with LLM agents emphasize modular design and various defensive mechanisms against adversarial attacks [3][4][5].
- Applications of LLM agents span multiple sectors including government, healthcare, and retail, revealing notable efficiencies but also challenges regarding accuracy and privacy [6].

## Background
LLM agents are AI systems designed to facilitate complex tasks through language comprehension and action-oriented frameworks. With increasing adoption, understanding their definitions, frameworks, and functionalities is essential for their effective utilization [1]. Recent advancements in the field underscore the importance of integrating these agents seamlessly into various operational contexts [2].

## Foundational Definitions
LLM agents are categorized based on their capabilities, including reflex and learning agents. They are characterized by an architecture that typically comprises a decision-making core, memory modules for contextual understanding, and planning components that guide their actions [1][7][8]. Central concepts such as agentic AI clarify distinctions in agent behavior, underscoring the necessity of tool integration and task adaptability [2].

## Major Methods and Frameworks for Tool Integration
Various frameworks have emerged to facilitate effective LLM agent operations, focusing on aspects such as modularity, trajectory design, and defense against adversarial threats [3][4]. Notable frameworks include:
- **PLUTO**: A tool designed for trajectory planning in missions [4].
- **ScaleMCP**: Enhances tool selection via dynamic contextual adaptations [9].
These frameworks are pivotal for building robust applications capable of evolving through user interaction and operational needs.

## Trends and Open Problems
The landscape for LLM agents continues to evolve rapidly. Noteworthy observations include a shift towards treating LLMs as essential components in automation strategies. For example, a significant portion of AI deployments now focus on autonomous task execution [6]. However, challenges regarding data privacy, regulatory compliance, and the accuracy of outputs remain pertinent, hampering broad acceptance and reliable use across sectors [6]. Addressing these issues is critical for the future of LLM agent adoption and functionality.
