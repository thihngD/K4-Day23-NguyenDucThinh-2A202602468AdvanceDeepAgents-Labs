# Survey about World Model

## TL;DR
- World models are internal representations used by AI systems to predict environmental changes [1].
- Various methodologies are employed in their development, addressing architecture and decision-making processes [2][3].
- Applications of world models range from autonomous driving systems to enhancing reasoning in language models [4][5].

## Background
World models in AI represent the concept of creating internal simulations of an environment to predict future states and enhance decision-making. The foundational work in this area began in the 1990s and has gained traction due to advancements in neural networks and autonomous systems [1][6].

## Foundational Concepts of World Models
World models serve as a predictive tool that allows machines to learn and simulate their environments efficiently. They are categorized broadly into explicit and implicit models, with the latter being essential for scalable applications [7]. Key elements include their capability to capture dynamic environments and their roots in cognitive science, which provide theoretical insights into how agents interact with their surroundings [8][9].

## Major Methodologies in World Model Development
Different architectures characterize the methodologies used in constructing world models. A recent survey proposes a taxonomy across four key dimensions: architecture, methodology, reasoning strategy, and application domain [2]. There is an ongoing discourse on the integration of various design principles to optimize decision-making capabilities within these models [3][10]. Moreover, critiques highlight critical factors for creating general-purpose models, including training data selection and representation design [3].

## Applications and Implications
World models have applications across diverse domains, from generative AI to robotics. They significantly impact AI policy training in simulated environments and improve interpretability in language models [4][5]. As world models evolve, their implications for AI capabilities and real-world tasks become more evident, indicating a shift towards more intelligent systems capable of complex decision-making [5].

## Trends and Open Problems
In recent years, the development of world models has evolved significantly with new approaches, including level x laws taxonomy, offering insights into the governing laws of AI systems [10]. However, challenges remain in ensuring robustness and functionality, particularly in dynamic real-world scenarios, suggesting areas for future research.
